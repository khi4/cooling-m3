How to read this. The shaded band between the two curves is the product. It widens as the run proceeds, because the bare machine keeps soaking heat into a desk that cannot absorb it fast enough. Where a curve flattens at 100 °C on the chip view, that machine has started throttling — it is holding temperature by doing less work, which the “work sustained” figure shows.

What is being solved. Two transient cases step together. Solids are lumped RC nodes — chip, chassis, and twenty slices of the stand along the flow path. Air is marched slice by slice. Channel flow comes from a pressure balance: buoyancy against viscous loss when passive, fan curve against system curve when active. The desk is a semi-infinite solid under transient flux, which is why a steel desk behaves nothing like oak. One constant is fitted so a bare machine on wood reaches 46 °C case and 95 °C chip after 45 minutes at 16 W and 22 °C. Not CFD — it resolves temperature along the channel, not across the machine, so no hot spots and no recirculation. Compare configurations with it; do not quote absolute numbers from it.
